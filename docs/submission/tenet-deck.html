02 · The insightidea & innovation 30 · practical applicability 20

Trust is not a setting.
It is the ability to revoke.

Permission today is granted once and lives forever — a key in production. What is missing is not another view. It is the moment a permission ends.

  • Four barriers, one cause. Culturally an agent is not trusted as a colleague; socially it is “if it happens, it is on me”; product-wise there is nothing to stop with; in the story we tell ourselves, “we see everything already”.
  • The old way: a log you read on Monday — evidence after the fact, never authority before it.
  • So the unit is not visibility. It is authorized action — and the receipt that proves it.

03 · The ideaidea & innovation 30 · relation to category 20

No approval, no action. TENET holds that one rule.

Every tool-call passes the kernel before it executes: scope, TTL, signer. One click takes the authority back — and the agent stops mid-flight, not after the export.

Authority
Identity is bound to one agent and one grant. A grant for one order never authorizes another.
Grant
HMAC-SHA256 over the scope. The signature is verified at the gate, before any rule is read.
Revoke
One call halts the agent mid-flight. The next call returns REVOKED, not “unknown”.
Receipt
Append-only, hash-chained proof: who authorized, under which order, how long it lived.
  • Every call is either rejected before execution or carries a receipt.
  • Revocable by design — the order exists to be taken back, not to be filed.
